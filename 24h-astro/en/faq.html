FAQ

Where does my location come from? From your current activity, or the last activity recorded with GPS. The watch face keeps it in memory. You can also enter a fixed location in the settings ("Manual position"), in decimal degrees.

Why the crossed-out location icon? Why Paris? No location has been obtained yet: the watch face computes for Paris. Start an activity with GPS, even for a few seconds, or turn on "Manual position" and enter your coordinates. The icon disappears within the next quarter of an hour.

Are the times accurate? Yes: sunrise, sunset and twilights to the minute, moonrise and moonset within a few minutes, checked against reference ephemerides (JPL, USNO). The times shown are for your location, not for a nearby city.

Does the compass point north? No, it is a fixed map: north at the top, east on the right. The triangles show the direction of the sun (or moon) on that map, not which way your wrist is facing.

Why doesn't the moon disc move at a steady speed? It is placed between the rise horizon and the set horizon: over the top of the ring when the moon is up, along the bottom when it is down. Its speed along the ring therefore changes over time.

The ring is all blue, or almost empty. Beyond the Arctic Circle, this is polar day (the sun never sets) or polar night (it never rises). The calculations are still right.

Can the time be shown in 12-hour format? No: it is a 24-hour watch face, and the time follows the same turn as the ring.

My sport total for the year looks low. On the day you install it, the watch face only knows the activities of the past 40 days; the total then fills up. See Watch data.
